2026 Salary Review — Executive Summary

Selective merit increase under zero-budget mandate  |  Calibrated against Italy IT market 2026

Confidential — Internal use only

Context

HR mandate: zero base salary increase for 2026. This algorithm selectively distributes resources to protect top performers and at-risk talent while remaining defensible to HR. All new base salaries (non-bottom) are rounded UP to the nearest €500 via CEILING. Bottom performers retain their exact current salary — zero exceptions.

Guiding Principles

  1. Reward performance rank
  2. Correct market gaps at lower salary levels
  3. Protect retention where it matters most
  4. Fully defensible under the EU Pay Transparency Directive (effective 7 June 2026)

Market Calibration

Algorithm calibrated against Italy IT market 2026: average tech sector growth 5–7% (Hays Salary Guide 2026, Feb 2026). Top performers intentionally receive above-market increases to correct accumulated gaps. Middle and Top 30% are within market range. Only gap: low-score Middle with salary > €55k.

4. MIDDLE — Score-Based Logic

Score Formula
Score = (Criticality × 0.6) + (Potential × 0.4)   |   Max = 6.0
Criticality weighted 60% — retention risk is immediate. Potential weighted 40% — represents future investment.
Salary-based floors protect against manager under-rating bias: < €40k → min 2.5%, €40–55k → min 1.5%, regardless of score.

6. Design Notes & Known Limitations

  1. Cliff edges at band thresholds (36k, 45k, 55k, 60k, 70k). Mathematically inherent with €500 CEILING rounding. Accepted — cases are rare and deltas minimal.
  2. Manager rating bias in Middle. Partially mitigated by salary floors. Full fix: calibration session before next cycle.
  3. Middle low score > €55k receives 0%. Only gap vs Italian IT market 2026 (expected 2–3%). Consider a 1% universal floor for middle ≤ €60k in next cycle.
  4. Ranking typo → salary unchanged silently. Add a validation column to flag unrecognized ranking values.
  5. EU Pay Transparency Directive effective 7 June 2026. This documented algorithm is the required written justification for pay dispersion between tiers. Keep it versioned.
